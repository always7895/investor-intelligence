# Leopold Aschenbrenner source view and system operationalization

> Primary source: Leopold Aschenbrenner, *Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead* (June 2024).
>
> Attribution boundary: the essay presents arguments about AI progress, infrastructure, security and geopolitics. The stock-screen taxonomy and numerical investment rules in this repository are project-authored operationalizations, not rules published or endorsed by Aschenbrenner.

## 1. Source-derived view

The official essay supports the following summary:

- Aschenbrenner argues that AGI by 2027 is "strikingly plausible", based on continued increases in physical compute, algorithmic efficiency and capability gains from unhobbling models into useful agents.
- He expects very large increases in AI investment, including GPUs, datacenters, power and associated physical infrastructure before the end of the decade.
- He argues that willingness to spend may not be the main binding constraint for the largest clusters; power, land, permitting and datacenter construction can have longer lead times.
- His cluster estimates are scenarios and rough trend extrapolations, not guarantees.
- The series also emphasizes AI-lab security, superalignment, national-security competition and the risk of a rapid intelligence explosion.

- series introduction and table of contents: https://situational-awareness.ai/
- compute, algorithmic efficiency and unhobbling argument: https://situational-awareness.ai/from-gpt-4-to-agi/

# System operationalization — not an Aschenbrenner stock-picking framework

For reproducible research, this repository maps the essay's infrastructure discussion into three project-authored research domains. These domains are analytical convenience only.

## Domain A: power and physical site constraints

Research subjects may include:

- generation and on-site power;
- grid interconnection and transmission;
- transformers, switchgear, cables and electrical equipment;
- land, permitting, cooling and datacenter construction;
- fuel supply and dispatchability.

Required questions:

- Is there primary evidence that AI/datacenter demand reaches this company's economics?
- What is the capacity, lead time and permitting constraint?
- Is the company a bottleneck, a commodity supplier or merely a thematic beneficiary?
- What capital, dilution and execution risk is required to expand?

## Domain B: compute platforms and networking

Research subjects may include:

- accelerators and custom silicon;
- cloud and neocloud capacity;
- datacenter networking and interconnect;
- systems integration and cluster operation.

Required questions:

- Are orders, deployments and customer commitments documented?
- How durable are scarcity and pricing power?
- What are the risks from hyperscaler internal silicon, customer concentration and financing?
- Are capacity and revenue timing being confused with announced headline value?

## Domain C: components and manufacturing constraints

Research subjects may include:

- memory and advanced packaging;
- optical connectivity and photonics;
- substrates, wafers, process equipment and test;
- foundry and manufacturing capacity.

Required questions:

- Is the claimed bottleneck supported by utilization, qualification, pricing, lead-time or contract evidence?
- What substitutes or architecture changes could remove the constraint?
- Can the company fund and execute the required capacity expansion?
- Does end-market growth translate into company-level revenue and cash flow?

## Project-authored evaluation factors

The system may score:

- demand alignment;
- constraint severity and replacement difficulty;
- company financial quality;
- valuation and dated catalysts;
- evidence quality, execution risk and disconfirmation.

All weights and thresholds are repository choices. Reports must label them `System operationalization`, not `Aschenbrenner score` or `Aschenbrenner recommendation`.

## Macro and risk context

The system should preserve the essay's broader scope by tracking:

- compute and algorithmic-efficiency trend changes;
- AI revenue and capex changes;
- power, land, permitting and construction constraints;
- export controls and national-security policy;
- model-security and alignment developments;
- evidence that scaling or commercialization is slower or faster than the essay's scenarios.

Contrary evidence must be reported, not suppressed to protect a thesis.

## Time-horizon rule

Aschenbrenner's essay is a scenario for the decade ahead. It is not a statement that every infrastructure security should be held for at least two years.

The repository owner's two-year-or-longer preference is calculated only in the separate `User-defined long-term suitability overlay` documented in `skills/user-long-term-overlay.md`.
