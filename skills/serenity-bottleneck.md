# System operationalization — not a verbatim Serenity framework

The repository uses the following seven research factors to translate recurring supply-chain themes into a testable process. Thresholds are configurable defaults and require evidence.

## Factor 1: demand-wave alignment

Check whether the company participates in a documented demand expansion rather than merely using an AI label.

Evidence examples:

- customer capex plans;
- named product ramps;
- capacity commitments;
- filings or earnings-call disclosures;
- credible industry roadmaps.

## Factor 2: bottleneck or chokepoint relevance

Determine whether the company controls scarce capacity, qualification, intellectual property, process knowledge or another difficult-to-replace link.

The system must distinguish:

- **bottleneck** — output is constrained by this link;
- **chokepoint** — the architecture or qualification process makes substitution difficult;
- **beneficiary** — demand may help the company, but the supply chain can function without it.

A category label alone is not proof.

## Factor 3: pricing power

Look for evidence of price increases, favorable contract terms, rising gross margins, scarcity rents or a small cost share relative to the customer's system value.

Gross margin is a proxy, not conclusive proof.

## Factor 4: substitution and qualification risk

Assess credible alternatives, customer qualification time, technical migration paths, internal sourcing and the time/cost required to switch.

The system must record the evidence for any claimed qualification period instead of assuming one from the product category.

## Factor 5: addressable-market expansion

Assess whether the relevant end market is expanding and whether the company can economically capture that growth.

TAM growth alone is insufficient; the report must also test share, capacity, pricing, customer mix and capital requirements.

## Factor 6: valuation and market-expectation gap

Assess whether the operating evidence differs from what appears priced into the security. Market cap, P/S, analyst coverage and institutional ownership are supporting observations, not Serenity-authored cutoffs.

## Factor 7: evidence quality

Use a provenance hierarchy:

1. **Primary/strong** — filings, named contracts, exchange announcements, official awards, capacity and timing disclosures, audited financial statements.
2. **Corroborating** — customer/supplier pages, adjacent-company calls, industry roadmaps, government documents and credible trade publications.
3. **Lead only** — social posts, unnamed rumors, mirror text and price action.

A social post can identify a lead but cannot independently prove the company's economics.

## System risk gates

These are project-authored checks, not Serenity's published formal exclusions:

1. **Dilution and financing** — ATM issuance, stock-based compensation, liquidity, debt maturity and expansion funding.
2. **Customer concentration** — single-customer and top-three exposure, contract duration and counterparty quality.
3. **Thesis deterioration** — revenue, backlog, margins, design wins, capacity, customer loss and changed product timelines.
4. **Jurisdiction and policy exposure** — export controls, sanctions, tariffs, geographic concentration and supply-chain relocation.

The system must not automatically remove a company when required data is missing. Missing evidence reduces confidence and triggers review.

## Time-horizon rule

Preserve the horizon of each Serenity post or thesis as published. Do not convert a company-specific short-term or long-term statement into a universal rule.

The repository owner's preference to evaluate suitability for holding at least two years is applied only in the separate `User-defined long-term suitability overlay` documented in `skills/user-long-term-overlay.md`.
